Post-Quantum Zcash

The soundness obligations behind Kishu: the statements

Statements drafted 2026-11-14, to be reviewed before any proof is attempted. Sources: the Booleanity-by-projection draft “Efficient Booleanity Checking via Structured Modular Projections” of 2026-09-23 (§2 Theorem 1, §4 equation 6, §5 the certificate), and spike/booleanity_spike.py (the limb protocol). The Pasta-claims Akita variant has a different obligation, which is not Kishu’s; it is the subject of The soundness obligation behind the Pasta-claims Akita variant.

1. Kishu’s obligations

Kishu’s claim field is Fq​ with q=264−59, which is Akita’s own 64-bit commitment modulus. There is no decoupling of the commitment modulus from the claim field: the bits are committed one per digit as any small digit vector is, and the evaluation claims are Akita’s ordinary claims at the 64-bit profile, over the quadratic extension E=Fq​[u]/(u2−2) that the draft’s sum-check uses. The Pasta values never reach the commitment: the first 255 bits of a value are read as eight 32-bit limbs vj​=⟨cj​,s⟩, proved as linear claims in the same sum-check, and the verifier recomposes V=∑j​232jvj​ over the integers and reduces modulo p.

So Kishu’s obligations are: (B1) Akita’s knowledge soundness at its 64-bit profile with claims in E, which the paper proves in the classical ROM; (B2) the soundness of the Booleanity check; (B3) an elementary limb lemma. The PCS frontier page’s Kishu paragraph once said that Kishu carries “the same open soundness obligation” as the Pasta-claims Akita variant, whose claims are Fp​ values over the 64-bit commitment. It was corrected on 2026-11-14 (commit c8aa3302edbb5377e925a85e6552223c53f1934b): that variant’s obligation, Theorem A on its own page, is Kishu’s fallback’s, not Kishu’s.

Which Booleanity check (B2) is about. The frontier’s Kishu rows use the conventional weighted sum-check, the draft’s §3 baseline, not its projection. In recursion the projection’s verifier is the merge circuit’s work, a committed digit per sampled coefficient at the least. The projection is therefore ahead only from rank 23 at the draft’s geometry. Separately, ranks of 21 and up are too slow for a Zcash transaction, which puts the frontier’s Kishu rows at 19 and 20, where the sum-check form wins. (B2) is then the ordinary sum-check’s soundness, with no integer lift. The draft’s projection certificate, with its CertifiedJL estimates and its numerical check, is not on Kishu’s path, so formally verifying the projection is not a blocker for Zcash’s use of Kishu (the user’s decision, 2026-11-17). The projection form’s statement is kept as Theorem B′ below, for a verifier that runs natively.

2. The no-wrap lemma

Lemma L (no wrap). Let F be a prime field of size r, and let u,v∈Z with ∣u∣,∣v∣<r/2. If u≡v(modr) then u=v.

This is the same Lemma L as on the Pasta-claims variant’s page, repeated so that each page stands alone; here only Theorem B′ needs it. Theorem 1 of the draft is Lemma L applied to u=Dx​=∑i​xi​(xi​−1) and v=0, with the bound 0≤Dx​≤Ex​+nEx​​<Q0​+nQ0​​<q from Cauchy–Schwarz.

3. Kishu’s statements

Lemma B3 (limbs). Let s∈{0,1}n with n≤231, and let cj​∈Zn have entries 2i−32j at positions i∈[32j,32j+32) and zero elsewhere. Then the integer vj​=⟨cj​,s⟩ satisfies 0≤vj​<232, so its value in Fq​ is its integer value, V=∑j​232jvj​ is the integer whose bits are s0​,…,s254​, and Vmodp is the Pasta value those bits encode.

Proof sketch: each vj​ is a sum of at most 32 distinct powers of two below 232, and 232<q; Lemma L is not even needed, the integer is below q outright. The hypothesis n≤231 is only what makes the general linear claims of equation 6 (the projection rows, entries in {−1,0,1}) have integer magnitude below q/2 as well.

Theorem B (Kishu’s claim reduction, sum-check form). Fix s∈Fqn​ authenticated by an Akita commitment at the 64-bit profile. Suppose the verifier accepts the sum-check over E, with uniform r∈Em and μ∈E8 drawn after the commitment, of a∑​eq(r,a)s(a)(s(a)−1)+s(a)w(a)=j∑​μj​vj​,w=j∑​μj​cj​, with the terminal evaluation v=s(ρ) authenticated by the commitment. Then, except with probability at most (4m+1)/∣E∣ over the verifier’s randomness, plus Akita’s knowledge error for the opening: s∈{0,1}n, every vj​ is the integer limb of Lemma B3, and Vmodp is the Pasta value whose bits s carries.

Where each term comes from: m/∣E∣ is the weighted defect ∑a​eq(r,a)sa​(sa​−1), a nonzero multilinear polynomial in r when some sa​∈/{0,1}, vanishing at a uniform r (Schwartz–Zippel); 1/∣E∣ is the affine batching of the limb claims by independent μj​ (if some vj​=⟨cj​,s⟩, the batched equation holds with probability exactly 1/∣E∣ over μ, whatever r is); 3m/∣E∣ is the degree-three sum-check; and the opening’s authentication is Akita’s. No integer lift is needed, since x(x−1)=0 in a prime field only at 0 and 1.

Theorem B′ (the projection form; not on Kishu’s path). Fix s∈Fqn​ authenticated by an Akita commitment at the 64-bit profile, before the projection randomness. Suppose the verifier accepts the protocol of the draft’s equation 6 extended by the limb terms (the spike’s steps 6 and 7): the batched quadratic sum-check over E of a∑​s(a)(s(a)−1)+s(a)w(a)=⟨λ,y⟩+j∑​μj​vj​,w=T⊤λ+j∑​μj​cj​, with the terminal evaluation v=s(ρ) authenticated by the commitment, and the integer energy checks on the revealed y. Then, except with probability at most εproj​+(2m+2)/∣E∣ over the verifier’s randomness, plus Akita’s knowledge error for the opening: s∈{0,1}n, y=Ts, every vj​ is the integer limb of Lemma B3, and Vmodp is the Pasta value whose bits s carries.

Where each term comes from: εproj​ is the draft’s equation 1, the projection’s lower-tail certificate (its 2−136 is conditional on CertifiedJL’s estimates (10) and (11)); (2m+1)/∣E∣ is the quadratic sum-check’s algebraic error from the draft’s §4; the extra 1/∣E∣ is the affine batching of the limb claims with the projection claims by independent μj​, which is the same argument as the draft’s for λ (if y=Ts or some vj​=⟨cj​,s⟩, the batched equation holds with probability exactly 1/∣E∣ over the batching vector); Booleanity is Theorem 1 from Ex​<Q0​ and the zero defect; and the opening’s authentication is Akita’s.

Neither Theorem B nor B′ covers:

  • the Fiat–Shamir transform, inherited from Akita’s ROM proof; the draft’s AES-derived cores are a computational assumption on top of B′;
  • zero knowledge: B′ has none, since the revealed y forgoes it, as the draft says, and B’s rows take Akita’s mask;
  • the accumulation: the fold’s sum-checks are Akita’s, over E at the 64-bit profile, unchanged by Kishu.

So Kishu’s soundness reduces to Akita’s at its native profile plus a standard sum-check argument. The decoupled commitment modulus of the Pasta-claims variant is not involved, and neither is the draft’s certificate.

4. What to do next, once the statements are reviewed

  1. Theorem B first, since it is Kishu’s. Its proof is the standard one: Schwartz–Zippel on the weighted defect, the affine batching, and the sum-check’s soundness. Add the sum-check form to the spike beside the projection form. Its adversarial cases are the ones the statement names: a non-Boolean s; a vj​ off by a multiple of q; and batching with a dependent μ. Add an exhaustive check of Lemma B3’s bound at small n and small q.
  2. Not a blocker: the independent recomputation of the draft’s certificate at its parameters, which is what εproj​=2−136 in Theorem B′ rests on beyond CertifiedJL’s estimates. It is needed only if the projection form is adopted for a native verifier.
  3. Lean: Lemma B3 first, then Theorem B’s reduction in VCVio. Lemma L is needed here only by Theorem B′, and the projection certificate only by B′.

Post-Quantum Zcash

The soundness obligation behind the Pasta-claims Akita variant: the statement

Statement drafted 2026-11-14, to be reviewed before any proof is attempted. Sources: §6.3 of the Akita proof sizes at a Pasta modulus page (the decoupled commitment modulus), and LaBinius (ePrint 2026/2103). Kishu’s obligations are different ones, the subject of The soundness obligations behind Kishu.

1. The design and its obligation

Akita with Pasta claims over the 64-bit commitment (pasta_q64). The claim field is Pallas’s field Fp​. The witness is the balanced base-8 digit vector of the Fp​ coefficients, 85 digits each, committed under Akita’s Ajtai matrix over Rqc​​=Zqc​​[X]/(X64+1) with qc​=264−59. The sum-checks, the partials and the claim values are Fp​ values. The ring relations modulo qc​ are checked over Fp​ with explicit quotient witnesses: Az−∑i​ci​ti​=qc​k over the integers, k carried as digits. This is the decoupling of §6.3, a protocol change whose knowledge-soundness proof has to be redone with the no-wrap step.

Kishu does not use this decoupling: its claim field is the commitment modulus itself, so its obligations are Akita’s own at the 64-bit profile, a Booleanity check, and a limb lemma, stated on its own page. Theorem A below is the obligation of Kishu’s fallback, the variant that keeps its claims in Fp​, not of Kishu.

2. The no-wrap lemma

Lemma L (no wrap). Let F be a prime field of size r, and let u,v∈Z with ∣u∣,∣v∣<r/2. If u≡v(modr) then u=v.

This is the same Lemma L as on the Kishu page, repeated so that each page stands alone. §6.3’s “an Fp​ identity among integers of size 286 cannot wrap” is Lemma L with r=p≈2254.

3. The statement

Theorem A (knowledge soundness of the decoupled Akita), to be stated precisely once the protocol is written. The protocol is Akita’s at the fp128-style profile, with these changes: the claim field is Fp​; every relation the verifier checks is an Fp​ identity; the ring relations Az=∑i​ci​ti​ modulo qc​ are replaced by Az−∑i​ci​ti​=qc​k with k a committed digit witness; the digit range and norm checks are Fp​ identities on the digits. The claim is: if Module-SIS over Rqc​​ holds at the accumulated bound β, Akita’s extractor yields, from any accepting prover, a digit vector w with ∥w∥2​≤β such that the committed polynomial’s coefficients are ∑k​8kwj,k​modp and the claimed evaluation holds in Fp​, with Akita’s knowledge error and no new probabilistic term.

The new step is deterministic: every Fp​-checked identity must have both sides of integer magnitude below p/2 for Lemma L to lift it, and the lifted ring relation must then hold modulo qc​. The magnitudes to pin:

  • The ring relation: ∣(Az)i​∣≤N⋅qc​⋅∥z∥∞​ for N digit columns, so with N=222 and the accumulated ∥z∥∞​≤227 that is 2113<p/2≈2253: fine. But the quotient k is then up to N⋅∥z∥∞​=249, 17 base-8 digits, where §6.3 says “eleven base-8 digits per relation coordinate” with “∣k∣ around 231”. The count depends on where in the tree the relation is checked: a fresh witness has digits at most 4, an accumulated one entries near the norm bound. The proof must fix the quotient’s width per level, and the page’s digit count for k may be low.
  • The digit range identity, e.g. ∏a=−43​(wi​−a)=0 summed with random weights: magnitude at most N⋅(∥w∥∞​+4)8≤222⋅2248, which is NOT below p/2 for accumulated witnesses at 227. The range check for accumulated witnesses must therefore be the norm check (an ℓ2​ sum of squares, magnitude N⋅254=276, fine), or the range identity must be applied only to fresh digits. This is the kind of detail that decides whether the design is sound as described.
  • The evaluation claim needs no lift: it is an Fp​ statement about the Fp​ reductions of the recomposed digits.

LaBinius (ePrint 2026/2103) proves the analogous decoupling with extraction over the number field, avoiding reduction modulo the commitment modulus; its claim field is binary. Its argument is the one to adapt, and the two bullets above are where the adaptation is not mechanical.

4. A cost consequence

In this design the fold’s norm and range sum-checks run over Fp​, since every sum-check message is an Fp​ value (§6.3). A linear-time sum-check prover pays about one full claim-field multiplication per digit per pass after the first fold: 32 word multiplications in Fp​, against 2 at the 64-bit commitment modulus. The page’s accumulation prover prices the sum-checks at the claim field’s rate, since the same commit of 2026-11-14. Before it the pasta_q64 rows were charged the commitment modulus’s rate, so their accumulation term was low by a factor of three to four. Kishu is unaffected: its sum-checks are over E at 64 bits, which is what the draft measured.

  2. Lean: Lemma L, then Theorem A’s reduction, once the protocol is written.

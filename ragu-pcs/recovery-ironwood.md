# Recovery from Ironwood

ZIP 2005 makes Ironwood-pool notes quantum-recoverable in the sense of [this section's definition](spend-authorization.md#quantum-recovery) [ZIP-2005](#ref-zip2005). Each note's keys commit to a quantum key $\mathsf{qk} = \mathsf{H^{qk}}(\mathsf{qsk})$, the quantum spending key $\mathsf{qsk}$ derived from the spending key and in no viewing key. A Recovery Statement, to be introduced in a later upgrade, spends such a note without discrete logarithms. The statement needs two authorizations of a recovery spend. One is a RedDSA signature under the randomized key, which FROST makes $t$-of-$n$. The other is $\mathsf{SoK^{qsk}}$: a signature of knowledge of $\mathsf{qsk}$ on the transaction's SigHash, with $\mathsf{H^{qk}}(\mathsf{qsk}) = \mathsf{qk}$, which is the [hash preimage](auth-preimage.md) method made as a signature. With $\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{true}$ the rest of the statement is proved from viewing-key material, the path a hardware wallet is meant to optimize and the one priced in this section. The other path, $\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{false}$, has the statement prove the key derivations from the spending key itself, and is not considered here. In a Zoodles transaction the recovery spends also reveal their total, for the [pool accounting](zoodles.md#pool-accounting) that the Zoodles page sets out.

With FROST, ZIP 2005 has the participants agree privately on the value $\mathsf{sk}$ from which $\mathsf{nk}$, $\mathsf{qsk}$, and $\mathsf{qk}$ are derived, while $\mathsf{ak}$ is the output of the distributed key generation. Every wallet then holds the same $\mathsf{qsk}$. So against an adversary who breaks discrete logarithms the recovery spend's unforgeability is that of $\mathsf{SoK^{qsk}}$ as a signature scheme with key $\mathsf{qk}$, 1-of-$n$, while a pre-quantum adversary corrupting up to $t - 1$ wallets is bounded by re-randomized FROST; [Hardware wallets](hardware-wallets.md) sets out that model. The frontier page prices the recovery circuit as an application: in Kishu with zero knowledge it takes <!--fig:tx_recovery_kishu_zk_s-->0.370<!--/fig--> s on one core at rank <!--fig:tx_recovery_kishu_zk_rank-->21<!--/fig-->.

## The signature method as an arm of the Recovery Statement

What a [leanSPHINCS](auth-leansphincs.md) route asks of ZIP 2005 is less than it first seems. Its $\mathsf{qk} = \mathsf{H^{qk}}(\mathsf{qsk})$ hashes the secret, and a hash-based signature needs $\mathsf{qk}$ to commit to the signature's public key instead. Deriving that key from the seed inside the Recovery Statement would cost the top layer's key generation, out of the question. But the derivation is domain-separated. A signature route would define its own derivation of $\mathsf{qk}$ from the public key under another context, and the Recovery Statement, which is not yet specified, would accept it as one more arm of its tagged union beside the preimage proof and $\mathsf{SoK^{sk}}$. The arms cannot satisfy each other's rule, since that would be a collision across two domain-separated hashes. So nothing has to be decided before the scheme is known. The one consequence is that a key derived before the signature route exists is a preimage-route key. A wallet that wants the signature route later needs a new key, which means new addresses and moved funds.

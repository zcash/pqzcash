# Regenerating the study's numbers

Every number on the PCS frontier page and in the two notes comes from one of three places: a
constant in `pareto-model.py` typed from a paper or a specification; a constant computed by a
script in `scripts/` from an external checkout; or the model's own arithmetic. This file says
how to redo the first two kinds. Paths are relative to `ragu-pcs/`.

## The page's figures and claims

`python pareto-model.py` writes `pareto.html`. Every figure that the text quotes sits between
markers in `pareto-template.html` and in the two notes, `pasta-proof-size.md` and
`zk-fiat-shamir-with-aborts.md`, as `<!--fig:NAME-->value<!--/fig-->`, and the model fills them
when it writes the page. The run fails when a marker's value in a
tracked file differs from the model's, or when one of the claims in `define_figures()` no longer
holds; so does the book build, which runs the model. Commands:

```
python pareto-model.py            # write pareto.html; fail on a stale figure or a failed claim
python pareto-model.py --fill     # also write the current values back into the tracked files
python pareto-model.py --figures  # list every figure
```

A new number in the text gets a `fig()` in `define_figures()` and a marker where it is quoted; a
qualitative statement that the model can check gets a `claim()`. A constant that a script
computes goes in the model with a comment naming the script and its inputs, as `RANK_SUMS_147`,
`WIDTH_SHRINK_147` and `WHIR_147` do.

## Pedersen-IPA

- **The proof size and the circuit budget** (`ragu-size-model.py`): the component structure is
  Ragu's wire format, and the gate and constraint counts are the ones that ragu_pcd's internal
  tests pin (`crates/ragu_pcd/src/internal/tests.rs` at 8109a41c). `python ragu-size-model.py`
  prints the checked-in proof, 7,908 bytes, and `python ragu-size-model.py budget` the budget
  table. After a Ragu change, read the pinned counts off those tests and update the constants at
  the top of the size model.
- **The fuse** (`PEDERSEN_FUSE_SECONDS`): in the Ragu checkout,
  `cargo bench -p ragu_pcd --features multicore --bench criterion_fuse`, the Merge2 step at the
  production rank with one worker, on one core, no assembly. The model calibrates the Pedersen
  prover to it.
- **Tachyon's circuits** (`TACHYON_STEPS`): apply `scripts/patches/ragu-counting-adapter.patch`
  to the Ragu worktree at a1395401, copy `scripts/tachyon-gates/` to `work/gates/` in the Tachyon
  checkout (its manifest points at `../../crates/tachyon` and at the worktree beside the
  checkout), and `cargo run` there. It prints each step's gates and constraints at header 9.
- **Halo 2** (`HALO2_SECONDS`): Orchard 0.16.0's `benches/circuit.rs` proving group with the
  recipient counts widened, built with rustc 1.100.0 and `pasta_curves` 0.6.1 with `asm`; the
  two-action and sixteen-action times on one thread.

## Akita

- **Openings, slopes, rows and the catalog report** (`AKITA_128`, `SLOPE[128]`, `ROWS`,
  `estimates/`): `scripts/akita-pasta-estimates.sh` builds the patched Akita and runs the rank
  ladder (`pasta-rank.csv`), the catalog re-pricing, the re-plans under each pricing override,
  and the 128-bit width tables for Pallas and Goldilocks. It writes the TSVs and CSVs that
  `estimates/` holds. The catalog security report there is `gen_schedule_artifacts --catalog-report`
  over the shipped families, built with the `catalog-security` feature, as the re-plan scripts run
  it. The per-K group runs behind the opening tables are not kept under `estimates/`; the re-plan
  scripts reproduce them.
- **The 147-bit floor** (`AKITA_147`, `SLOPE[147]`): `scripts/akita-147-pipeline.sh` retargets
  the worktree's policy, regenerates its width tables (hours; `SKIP_TABLES=1` resumes), re-sizes
  the compression cells, rebuilds and runs `scripts/akita-147-replan.sh`; then
  `python scripts/akita-150-page-inputs.py work/akita-147-out/replan` prints the two dicts in the
  model's shape. `scripts/akita-150-tables.sh` writes CSV width tables at a given floor for every
  modulus, and the Pallas table alone is
  `infinity_width_table --policy Quantum147BitADPS16 --profiles pallas --format csv --profile local-minimum --output ...`
  in the worktree: 5.5 hours with the estimator built without its `parallel` feature.
- **Rank growth** (`RANK_SUMS_147`): over the weakest instance of each of the catalog report's 115
  schedules, the least module rank that covers its width at each floor, mapped through each
  variant's modulus:

  ```
  python scripts/rank-scale.py estimates/catalog-security.tsv \
      work/akita-src/crates/akita-params/src/sis/generated_sis_table \
      work/akita-src-150/crates/akita-params/src/sis/generated_sis_table \
      estimates/linf-width-table-goldilocks.csv work/akita-147-out/linf-width-table-147-goldilocks.csv \
      estimates/linf-width-table-pallas.csv work/akita-147-out/linf-width-table-147-pallas.csv
  ```

- **Width shrink** (`WIDTH_SHRINK_147`): the median of the 128-bit width over the 147-bit one, per
  modulus and ring dimension, over the cells where neither width is at the estimator's search
  cap and the width falls. The shipped moduli come from the production table's policy audit at
  each floor, Pallas and Goldilocks from the CSV tables:

  ```
  python scripts/width-shrink.py 147 \
      work/akita-src/crates/akita-params/src/sis/generated_sis_table/policy_audit.csv \
      work/akita-src-150/crates/akita-params/src/sis/generated_sis_table/policy_audit.csv \
      pallas=estimates/linf-width-table-pallas.csv:work/akita-147-out/linf-width-table-147-pallas.csv \
      goldilocks=estimates/linf-width-table-goldilocks.csv:work/akita-147-out/linf-width-table-147-goldilocks.csv
  ```

- **Typed from the paper and the notes**: the verifier times (`AKITA_VERIFY_MS`, Table 16), the
  terminal's inner-image coordinates (`IMAGE_COORDS`, pasta-proof-size.md §6.1), and the mask's
  parameters (`MASK_*`, the zero-knowledge note's §5).

## WHIR, FRI and the limb hybrid

- **The sweeps** (`estimates/whir-sweep.tsv`, `estimates/whir-sweep-grind.tsv`): the reference
  binary at 2^17, 2^18 and 2^20 variables over `Field256` and `Goldilocks3`, rates 1/2 to 1/64,
  maximum proof of work 0, 16, 20 and 24 bits, 128 bits in the Johnson regime, three verifier
  repetitions:

  ```
  target/release/main -l 128 -d 17 -r 3 -f Field256 -p 20 --reps 3
  ```

  Each run prints `Proof size`, `Average hashes` and `Prover time`, which are the TSV's
  `proof_KiB`, `verifier_khashes` and `prover_ms`; `first_samples` and `queries` are the first
  round's sample count and the sum over rounds from its `commit ... samples N` lines.
- **The higher floor** (`WHIR_147`): `scripts/whir-sweep-147.sh` runs the same binary at 128 and
  147 bits over both fields at rates 1/2 to 1/8; the model takes the mean ratios.
- **Zero knowledge** (`ZK_BYTES`, `ZK_HASHES`, `ZK_PROVER`): Plonky3's HVZK-WHIR benchmark,
  `RUSTFLAGS="-Ctarget-cpu=native" cargo bench --bench zk_overhead -p p3-whir -- whir_zk_overhead`
  in `work/plonky3-src`.
- **FRI's factors** over WHIR (1.95 on the proof, 2.07 on the hashes) are the WHIR paper's
  measurements, typed.

## The 𝔽₂ family

`frib_optimizer.py` is the tracked port of Ulrich Haböck's FRI-Binius proof-size optimizer and
runs inside the model. Its calibration (`FRIB_MEASURED_MS`) and Flock's throughput and shares are
typed from the two papers.

## BitZ

`BITZ_T10` is the paper's Table 10, typed. The scalings measured with the proof of concept
(`BITZ_128`, `BITZ_RATE`, `BITZ_PHASES`) come from `scripts/bitz-rerun.sh build`, then
`scripts/bitz-rerun.sh <profile> 25-28 5` for the three profiles: `custom:1:4`, Table 10's rate 1/2 at
100 bits; `udrg:1:4:128`, rate 1/2 at 128 bits in the unique-decoding regime with grinding; and
`udrg:3:4:128`, rate 1/8 at 128 bits. Then
`python scripts/bitz-summary.py work/bitz-out/rerun/*.log` for the table of commit, grand-product,
Ligerito and verifier times and proof bytes. `scripts/bitz-pcs-sweep.sh` and
`scripts/bitz-overnight.sh` are the single-thread and ten-thread sweeps of the same benchmark.

`BINARY_MEASURED` is the Akita development branch's measurement of a binary-claim opening

## The applications

The recovery circuit's and the Merkle path's costs in each arithmetization (`RECOVERY_*`,
`RECOVERY_PATH_*`, `BOOL_*`) are derived in the model's comments from ZIP 2005's cost section and
the arithmetizations' own gate costs; Tachyon's nodes are the measured circuits above. Nothing
external is needed beyond the gate probe.

## The notes' own tables

The tables in `pasta-proof-size.md` §3, §3.1, §6.2 and §6.3 are the planner's figures from
`estimates/catalog-reprice.tsv` and `estimates/reprice-*.tsv`, which `scripts/akita-pasta-estimates.sh`
writes; the Pedersen figures there are `ragu-size-model.py`'s. The figures that the model also
computes carry markers and refill with `--fill`.

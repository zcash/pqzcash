# leanSPHINCS

The second authorization method is a stateless hash-based signature on a digest of the transaction, made outside the proof and verified inside the host's transaction. The signature and the public key are witnesses, which the zero-knowledge fold hides, so spends stay unlinkable and no re-randomized signature is needed. The scheme is leanSPHINCS, the signature of post-quantum Ethereum [EIP-8288](#ref-eip8288) [Lit26](#ref-lit26). A signature is <!--fig:leansphincs_sig_bytes-->4,276<!--/fig--> bytes with [Wam26](#ref-wam26)'s pruned key of lifetime $2^{30}$, the configuration priced here, <!--fig:leansphincs_layered_sig_bytes-->6,332<!--/fig--> with its layered forest key, and <!--fig:leansphincs_ethereum_bytes-->7,740<!--/fig--> with Ethereum's provisional set. Signing is hashing alone. The pruned key adds <!--fig:leansphincs_grind-->16,384<!--/fig--> grinding digests per signature, and its proved lifetime is <!--fig:leansphincs_lifetime-->126,900<!--/fig--> signatures, one per transaction. The grind is the signer's, since it is keyed and the key cannot leave the signer without revealing which leaves it kept; a full tree with a helper building and storing it has no grind, which [Hardware wallets](hardware-wallets.md) takes up.

## The message

Since the signature is made outside the proof, its message has to bind the transaction's effecting data. In Zoodles it is a BLAKE3 digest of them: each spend's two nullifiers, each output's commitment, the change commitment, the fee, and the epoch, <!--fig:zoodles_sighash_bytes-->235<!--/fig--> bytes or <!--fig:zoodles_sighash_blake3-->4<!--/fig--> compressions. The digest is a PCD header value: each spend verifies its signature over it, and the fee step recomputes it from the nullifiers and commitments that its children's headers carry. The verification, not the digest, is the expensive part of the method. In ZIP 2005's Recovery Statement [ZIP-2005](#ref-zip2005) the message is the SigHash.

## The verifier in the circuit

The host proves the verifier inside its transaction: <!--fig:leansphincs_blake2s_calls-->298<!--/fig--> BLAKE2s compressions or <!--fig:leansphincs_keccak_calls-->262<!--/fig--> Keccak-f permutations for the pruned key, against <!--fig:leansphincs_layered_blake2s_calls-->462<!--/fig--> and <!--fig:leansphincs_layered_keccak_calls-->408<!--/fig--> for the layered one and <!--fig:leansphincs_ethereum_perms-->515<!--/fig--> permutations for Ethereum's. XOR is not free in a digit arithmetization, so in Kishu a Keccak-f permutation is <!--fig:keccak_bits-->154,000<!--/fig--> committed bits against <!--fig:blake2s_bits-->21,300<!--/fig--> for a BLAKE2s compression. <!--claim:leansphincs_keccak_dearer:8d35d3-->The verifier is therefore <!--fig:app_leansphincs_blake2s_kishu-->6.39 million<!--/fig--> digits with BLAKE2s and <!--fig:app_leansphincs_keccak_kishu-->40.3 million<!--/fig--> with Keccak, <!--fig:leansphincs_keccak_over_blake2s-->6.3<!--/fig--> times as many, which is Ethereum's open hash question seen from the circuit.<!--/claim--> In a Boolean circuit with XOR free the ratio is <!--fig:leansphincs_keccak_over_blake2s_boolean-->3.3<!--/fig-->. With zero knowledge Kishu proves the BLAKE2s verifier in <!--fig:tx_leansphincs_blake2s_kishu_zk_s-->0.297<!--/fig--> s on one M4 core at rank <!--fig:tx_leansphincs_blake2s_kishu_zk_rank-->21<!--/fig--> and the Keccak one in <!--fig:tx_leansphincs_keccak_kishu_zk_s-->1.37<!--/fig--> s at rank <!--fig:tx_leansphincs_keccak_kishu_zk_rank-->22<!--/fig-->, beside <!--fig:tx_recovery_kishu_zk_s-->0.370<!--/fig--> s for the recovery circuit itself. Ethereum's draft fixes Keccak-256 as the scheme's only primitive; the circuit and the device both argue for BLAKE2s.

# Hardware wallets and the recovery statement

ZIP 2005's Recovery Statement needs two authorizations of a recovery spend: a RedDSA signature under the randomized key, which FROST makes $t$-of-$n$, and $\mathsf{SoK^{qsk}}$, a signature of knowledge of the quantum spending key $\mathsf{qsk}$ on the transaction's SigHash, with $\mathsf{H^{qk}}(\mathsf{qsk}) = \mathsf{qk}$ [ZIP-2005]. Its Option A keeps $\mathsf{qsk}$ on a hardware wallet, which makes $\mathsf{SoK^{qsk}}$ there, and the networked host proves the rest of the statement. This page asks what the wallet can prove and at what cost, with the figures of [the PCS frontier page](proof-systems/pcs-frontier.md), which prices the wallet's part as applications of its transaction measure, and how the signature of knowledge is argued. It was written on 2026-11-21 alongside an analysis of FROST members recovering from a seed phrase, from which it takes the device split.

## 1. The device split

The model to reason in makes the hardware wallets the only honest parties and treats every networked device as the adversary. A wallet holds the spending key $\mathsf{sk}$, or with FROST its signing share and the common $\mathsf{sk}$, hence $\mathsf{qsk}$. It exports $\mathsf{nk}$ and $\mathsf{qk}$ and nothing else secret-derived; $\mathsf{ak}$ is derived from $\mathsf{sk}$ and exported by a single-signer wallet, and with FROST it is the output of the distributed key generation. $\mathsf{rivk\_ext}$ and the incoming and outgoing viewing keys follow, so the host can scan, build transactions and produce the main Recovery proof, whose witness is viewing-key material plus the note and its path. ZIP 2005's other path, $\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{false}$, in which the Recovery Statement proves the key derivations from $\mathsf{sk}$ itself, is not considered here: $\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{true}$ is the path whose costs a hardware wallet is meant to optimize. The wallet signs FROST shares and makes $\mathsf{SoK^{qsk}}$, each after verifying the transaction and asking the user, and the user's confirmation is the adversary's choice of message. Two adversary classes share one win condition, a consensus-valid recovery spend on an unapproved SigHash: a pre-quantum one, with up to $t - 1$ wallets corrupted, bounded by the unforgeability of re-randomized FROST; and a discrete-log-breaking one, given every host's state and every network message but no wallet, bounded by the unforgeability of $\mathsf{SoK^{qsk}}$ as a signature scheme with key $\mathsf{qk}$, which is 1-of-$n$ since every wallet holds the same $\mathsf{qsk}$. Option A is the statement that the second class learns nothing from a wallet but its interface's outputs.

## 2. What the wallet can prove

Two shapes of $\mathsf{SoK^{qsk}}$ are priced. The wallet can prove its statement as a leaf of the transaction's own proof system, Kishu on the frontier page: one BLAKE3 compression, <!--fig:app_qk_bits-->15,100<!--/fig--> committed digits, the linking commitment of ZIP 2005's hardware-wallet section left out for now. Or it can sign SigHash with a stateless hash-based signature and leave the verification to the host, which proves it inside its own transaction with the signature and the public key as witnesses, so that recovery spends stay unlinkable.

### 2.1 A Kishu leaf

The handoff is the whole question. The host folds the wallet's leaf into the transaction's accumulator, and split accumulation needs the witness of what it folds, so the wallet hands over a masked witness: the response $z = y + cs$ of the [zero-knowledge note](proof-systems/working-notes/zk-fiat-shamir-with-aborts.md), a Gaussian mask $y$ added to the leaf's digits under a challenge $c$, accepted by rejection sampling so that $z$ is independent of the secret. The transaction measure charges the user's handoff a full merge, the proof that the fold is correct, and for a one-compression leaf that merge is most of the cost: the measure's <!--fig:tx_qk_kishu_zk_s-->0.109<!--/fig--> s at rank <!--fig:tx_qk_kishu_zk_rank-->15<!--/fig--> is <!--fig:tx_qk_kishu_zk_merges_pct-->48<!--/fig--> percent merge and <!--fig:tx_qk_kishu_zk_fold_pct-->47<!--/fig--> percent fold, the circuit itself a few percent.

The wallet need not prove that fold. The note's §4.2 lists the handoff merge's witness as simulatable, the hiding commitment, the mask's commitment, the challenge, the claims and the obligation transcripts, so the host can derive the challenge, prove the fold's merge and the obligations (the note's §5.8 deferral) and verify the leaf's sum-checks in its own merge. The wallet keeps what touches the secret. It commits the leaf with a hiding padding at the masked rank. It proves its constraint and Booleanity sum-checks under Libra-style masking polynomials, <!--fig:qk_sumcheck_mask_elements-->120<!--/fig--> committed elements, since the host sees their transcripts. It samples and commits the mask in idle time, and computes the response with the rejection test. The padding is computational rather than the note's leftover-hash one: $A\rho$ for $\rho$ of <!--fig:hiding_padding_bits-->5,000<!--/fig--> uniform bits is pseudorandom if knapsack-LWE is hard at the commitment's rows, which the primal estimate at the page's block size puts at about 3,300 LWE dimensions for binary error at the 64-bit modulus, a figure the lattice estimator should confirm; the statistical padding would be <!--fig:hiding_padding_statistical_kbits-->89<!--/fig--> thousand bits, six times the statement.

So the wallet commits <!--fig:qk_device_digits-->27,800<!--/fig--> digits: <!--fig:qk_device_online_ms-->0.603<!--/fig--> ms of online work on one M4 core, <!--fig:qk_device_idle_ms-->0.836<!--/fig--> ms in idle time, and a response of <!--fig:qk_device_z_kbytes-->76 kbytes<!--/fig--> at <!--fig:qk_device_z_bits-->22<!--/fig--> bits a coordinate. At <!--fig:device_mcu_rate-->10 million<!--/fig--> word multiplications a second for an MCU-class device and <!--fig:device_se_rate-->3 million<!--/fig--> for a secure element, assumptions scaled from the hash rates that [Wam26]'s hardware-wallet benchmark measured on a Ledger Nano S+, the computation takes <!--fig:qk_device_mcu_s-->0.18<!--/fig--> and <!--fig:qk_device_secure_element_s-->0.60<!--/fig--> s. The transfer of $z$ at that device's measured <!--fig:device_link_kbytes_s-->21<!--/fig--> kbytes a second over USB takes <!--fig:qk_device_link_s-->3.6<!--/fig--> s, <!--fig:qk_device_link_pipelined_s-->1.3<!--/fig--> s with a pipelined transport. No merge circuit and no accumulator sit on the device, and the witness is 13 kbytes as bits. So a Kishu signature of knowledge is within reach of a high-end hardware wallet on a USB link, the transfer being the larger part of it.

### 2.2 Why not an animated QR code

An airgapped wallet of the Keystone kind exchanges data by animated QR codes, fountain-coded fragments of a few hundred bytes a frame at a few frames a second, so a few kilobytes a second at best, with a phone camera on the wallet's screen. The response does not fit that channel, and the reason is not an encoding choice. <!--claim:qk_qr_channel_too_big:5e4200-->The accepted $z$ is a Gaussian of width $\sigma$ on every coordinate of the leaf, about $\log_2(\sigma\sqrt{2\pi e})$ bits of information each, and secret-dependent, so it cannot be regenerated from a seed as the mask can; with no padding and no masking elements the leaf's own <!--fig:app_qk_bits-->15,100<!--/fig--> coordinates still make <!--fig:qk_z_floor_kbytes-->40 kbytes<!--/fig-->, and a succinct Kishu proof of the leaf in place of the response would be about <!--fig:qk_succinct_proof_kbytes-->68 kbytes<!--/fig-->, nearly all of it the opening's fixed parts, the compression levels and the terminal, whatever the rank from <!--fig:qk_min_rank-->14<!--/fig--> up.<!--/claim--> A narrower mask saves two or three bits a coordinate for idle-time attempts, and a lighter challenge set a fraction of a bit. The transfer cannot be avoided by moving the fold elsewhere: split accumulation needs the host to hold a witness of the wallet's accumulator, and that witness is $z$. So a Kishu-style proof cannot be made small enough for a QR channel; a wallet on such a channel needs the signature of §2.3.

### 2.3 A hash-based signature

leanSPHINCS, the stateless hash-based signature of post-quantum Ethereum [EIP-8288] [Lit26], fits the QR channel: <!--fig:leansphincs_pruned_sig_bytes-->4,276<!--/fig--> bytes with a pruned key, <!--fig:leansphincs_sig_bytes-->6,332<!--/fig--> with [Wam26]'s layered forest key of lifetime $2^{30}$, and <!--fig:leansphincs_ethereum_bytes-->7,740<!--/fig--> with Ethereum's provisional set, a few seconds of scanning. Its signing is hashing alone, measured on a Nano S+ by [Wam26]'s benchmark, and it is stateless and deterministic, which matters when every member's wallet holds the same seed: a stateful scheme's index would be reused across wallets, and the deterministic randomizer makes the members' signatures identical. The host proves the verifier inside its transaction: <!--fig:leansphincs_blake2s_calls-->462<!--/fig--> BLAKE2s compressions or <!--fig:leansphincs_keccak_calls-->408<!--/fig--> Keccak-f permutations for the forest key, <!--fig:leansphincs_ethereum_perms-->515<!--/fig--> permutations for Ethereum's. In Kishu a Keccak-f permutation is <!--fig:keccak_bits-->154,000<!--/fig--> committed bits against <!--fig:blake2s_bits-->21,300<!--/fig--> for a BLAKE2s compression, since XOR is not free in a digit arithmetization, so the verifier is <!--fig:app_leansphincs_blake2s_kishu-->9.90 million<!--/fig--> digits with BLAKE2s and <!--fig:app_leansphincs_keccak_kishu-->62.7 million<!--/fig--> with Keccak, <!--fig:tx_leansphincs_blake2s_kishu_zk_s-->1.38<!--/fig--> against <!--fig:tx_leansphincs_keccak_kishu_zk_s-->6.39<!--/fig--> s with zero knowledge on one M4 core, beside <!--fig:tx_recovery_kishu_zk_s-->1.44<!--/fig--> s for the recovery circuit itself. Ethereum's draft fixes Keccak-256 as the scheme's only primitive; the circuit and the device both argue for BLAKE2s.

What this asks of ZIP 2005 is less than it first seems. Its $\mathsf{qk} = \mathsf{H^{qk}}(\mathsf{qsk})$ hashes the secret, and a hash-based signature needs $\mathsf{qk}$ to commit to the signature's public key instead, since deriving that key from the seed inside the Recovery Statement would cost the top layer's key generation, out of the question. But the derivation is domain-separated, so a signature route would define its own derivation of $\mathsf{qk}$ from the public key under another context, and the Recovery Statement, which is not yet specified, would accept it as one more arm of its tagged union beside the preimage proof and $\mathsf{SoK^{sk}}$. The arms cannot satisfy each other's rule, since that would be a collision across two domain-separated hashes. So nothing has to be decided before the scheme is known. The one consequence is that a key derived before the signature route exists is a preimage-route key, and a wallet that wants the signature route later needs a new key, that is new addresses and moved funds.

## 3. The signature of knowledge

A proof of knowledge of a preimage of $\mathsf{qk}$ is not enough: it would verify under any transaction, so it could be replayed. The statement has to be signed. The reason goes deeper than replay. The reduction that proves $\mathsf{SoK^{qsk}}$ unforgeable, as a signature scheme with key $\mathsf{qk}$, has to answer signing queries without $\mathsf{qsk}$, so it simulates them, and must then extract a preimage from a forger who has seen simulated proofs. That is simulation extractability, which plain knowledge soundness does not give, and a signature of knowledge is by definition a simulation-extractable proof with the message as its label [CL06].

### 3.1 The folklore technique, and where it is proved

The technique is to bind the message into the Fiat–Shamir transcript from the start: the instance and the message go into the hash before the first challenge, and the chain of challenges binds everything after. Classically this is proved. For sigma protocols it is [FKMV12]. For multi-round sum-check protocols it is recent: [DG23] shows Spartan and Bulletproofs simulation-extractable with no change, and [FFKRZ23] and [KPT23] prove it for Fiat–Shamir of a polynomial IOP with a binding commitment in general, under the conditions one expects, the instance hashed first and a unique-response or state-restoration property of the protocol. The Frozen Heart vulnerabilities of 2022 [TOB22], forgeries against implementations that left the instance out of the hash, are what omitting it costs. The technique is free.

What is missing is the quantum random-oracle model, which the Recovery Protocol needs. Fiat–Shamir of sigma protocols is settled there, [DFMS19] and [LZ19]. Knowledge soundness of Fiat–Shamir on an IOP is settled with polynomial loss, [CMS19]. But the multi-round measure-and-reprogram bound [DFM20] loses a factor exponential in the number of rounds, which for thirty sum-check rounds says nothing, and the simulation-extractability proofs above rewind, which the quantum setting does not allow. We know of no simulation-extractability result for a sum-check-based succinct argument in the quantum random-oracle model with usable bounds. In the trust-boundary style of the Ironwood formalization, the signature-of-knowledge property would have to be a named hypothesis.

### 3.2 A route that is available: the non-injectivity of the hash

A classical argument avoids simulation extractability altogether, and it fits this statement unusually well. $\mathsf{H^{qk}}$ maps 256 bits to 256 bits, so a uniformly random $\mathsf{qsk}$ has a second preimage with probability about $1 - 1/e$, <!--fig:sok_second_preimage_pct-->63<!--/fig--> percent. The reduction to the collision resistance of $\mathsf{H^{qk}}$ picks $\mathsf{qsk}$ itself, answers every signing query with the real prover, and extracts a preimage from the forgery. A zero-knowledge proof is witness indistinguishable [FS90], so when a second preimage exists the forger's view is the same whichever preimage the reduction holds, and the extracted one differs from the reduction's with probability about a half; then the reduction holds a collision. <!--claim:sok_loss_small:10a6c1-->The loss is a factor of about <!--fig:sok_loss_factor-->3.2<!--/fig-->.<!--/claim--> This is the two-witness form that [KW03] used for tight signature reductions, with the hash's own non-injectivity standing in for the second key, so the circuit does not change. It needs zero knowledge and knowledge soundness with auxiliary input, the honest signatures on other messages being the auxiliary input, both available in the quantum random-oracle model for the proof systems in play, and the message still bound into the instance before the first challenge. Nothing in the protocol changes; only the proof does. This is the proof to write for the Kishu route. The hash-based signature route does not raise the question: leanSPHINCS is a signature scheme with its own strong unforgeability proof, 127 classical bits in the random-oracle model [Wam26], and the message enters its digest with the randomizer.

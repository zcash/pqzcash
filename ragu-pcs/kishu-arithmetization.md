# Kishu's arithmetization

Kishu is a proof-carrying-data scheme in Ragu's shape. An application is a tree of nodes. Each node is a circuit that takes the public headers of its input nodes, checks whatever the application needs, and publishes a header of its own. The scheme attests that every node in the tree was satisfied, so that a verifier checks one proof for the whole tree. What differs from Ragu is what a node's circuit is written over. This page describes a node's circuit as its writer sees it, then the pieces Kishu is built from, the costs the [frontier page](pareto-template.html#kishu) prices it by, and the differences from Ragu's arithmetization. Every figure is the model's ([Regenerating the Study's Numbers](REGENERATING.md)).

## Kishu as a Proof-Carrying-Data scheme

A node's circuit has:

- a witness, the values its prover knows;
- a header, the values the node publishes, which the scheme carries to the node above; and
- the headers of its input nodes, which the scheme attests.

The circuit's writer states constraints over these three, and the scheme guarantees that a proof of the node exists only if the constraints hold and the input nodes were proved. A transaction is formed from a tree of nodes, as in Tachyon: a node for each spend and each output, nodes that combine them, and a node that fixes the fee, with the root's proof the transaction's. (For efficiency we distinguish nodes in the PCD graph from steps of the accumulation scheme, discussed below: a step is what the prover commits at once, and several nodes can share one.)

Every value a Kishu circuit holds is a vector of bits. A word of $w$ bits is a linear form in its bits, which costs nothing to form; a 64-bit field element is 64 bits; a 256-bit hash output is 256. The scheme proves once, for the whole witness, that every bit is 0 or 1, so the circuit's writer never states Booleanity. A range check is free: a value held in $w$ bits is below $2^w$ by construction. What the writer pays for is the bits themselves, one committed digit each, and the constraints.

A constraint is a polynomial relation among words: a sum of product terms, each the product of a few linear forms in the bits, with small or field constants, required to vanish in Kishu's native field, the 64-bit $\mathbb{F}_q$. Since a word is a linear form in certified bits, a relation whose terms stay below half of $q$ in absolute value holds over the integers whenever it holds in $\mathbb{F}_q$. That is how integer arithmetic is written. A 32-bit addition is $a + b = s + 2^{32} c$ with the sum bits and the carry committed, a comparison is a subtraction's carry, and arithmetic modulo another prime, such as Pasta's, is a relation with a committed quotient, as below. A multiplication of two words costs one product term; a constant costs by its nonzeros. Bit operations are a term each: XOR is $z = x + y - 2xy$ and AND is $z = xy$, each with its result bit committed; rotations and shifts are wiring and cost nothing. So a BLAKE3 compression is <!--fig:blake3_bits-->15,000<!--/fig--> digits and <!--fig:blake3_piop_terms-->7,904<!--/fig--> terms, a multiplication in $\mathbb{F}_q$ is one term over <!--fig:q64_digits-->64<!--/fig-->-digit operands, and a Pasta multiplication is <!--fig:bitz_gate_bits-->510<!--/fig--> or <!--fig:mult_limbs_digits-->1,088<!--/fig--> digits by one of two routes. The circuits that suit Kishu are the hash-heavy ones; the arithmetic of a large prime field is dear, since the field is not Kishu's own.

A step holds $2^{\mathrm{rank}}$ digits of witness, the rank chosen for the application between 13 and 24, and as many nodes as fit, as the [Zoodles](zoodles.md) page's packing shows. Zero knowledge asks nothing of the circuit's writer: the scheme hides the witness, and no randomizer or blinding wire is needed, though every value a node publishes must still be derived in its circuit, as the Zoodles page's section on binding a note to its action shows. Not available: native arithmetic in a field other than $\mathbb{F}_q$, and lookup arguments, which the study does not model.

## What Kishu is made of

Kishu is [BitZ](pareto-template.html#bitz)'s arithmetization over [Akita](pareto-template.html#akita)'s commitment scheme. Akita is a lattice-based polynomial commitment scheme, secure under the Module-SIS assumption: it commits to a vector of small integers, its digits, by an Ajtai hash over a ring with a 64-bit modulus, and it accumulates many such commitments into one by folding, so that a proof-carrying-data scheme can defer the expensive opening to the end. BitZ is a hash-based commitment scheme whose witnesses are bit-decomposed: it commits the bits and proves an evaluation claim over any ring by lifting it to the integers. Kishu takes BitZ's view of the witness, bits, and commits them with Akita, one bit per digit, so that Akita's accumulation replaces BitZ's hashing-heavy proofs.

The piece that such a combination needs is a Booleanity check over the lattice's own prime field, to certify that every digit is a bit. An internal research draft of September 2026, provisional and unreviewed, supplies one. The committed table's bits are certified by one unweighted quadratic identity plus an integer norm bound, which a structured random projection supplies by revealing 160 coordinates. So BitZ's exponent-lift GKR, <!--fig:bitz_gkr_share_pct-->79<!--/fig-->% of its prover, is not needed. The draft measures the reduction alone, single-threaded at $2^{27}$ bits over $2^{64} - 59$. The projection prover takes <!--fig:bool_projection_prover_ms-->113<!--/fig--> ms against <!--fig:bool_sumcheck_prover_ms-->438<!--/fig--> ms for the optimized conventional Booleanity sum-check, with verifiers of <!--fig:bool_projection_verify_ms-->0.85<!--/fig--> and <!--fig:bool_sumcheck_verify_ms-->0.023<!--/fig--> ms and payloads of <!--fig:bool_projection_kb-->2.6 kbytes<!--/fig--> and <!--fig:bool_sumcheck_kb-->1.7 kbytes<!--/fig-->. Neither includes the commitment opening or hiding. The projection form reveals coordinates of the committed table, so the rows that use it have no zero knowledge; the zero-knowledge rows use the sum-check form. The draft's certificates are conditional and not yet formalized; [Kishu's Soundness Obligations](kishu-soundness.md) states what has to be proved.

Beneath the step interface, Kishu accumulates as Ragu does: each step's proof is folded into an accumulator rather than verified, and a step's circuit is accompanied by a circuit that verifies the fold of its input proofs' accumulators, the merge, written in the same arithmetization. The sections below price the arithmetization's units, the merge, and the application circuits in them.

## Digits and constraints

Kishu commits one bit per digit over Akita's 64-bit modulus, plus the Booleanity check at <!--fig:bool_projection_per_bit-->2.5<!--/fig--> or <!--fig:bool_sumcheck_per_bit-->9.8<!--/fig--> word multiplications per bit, and a step commits $2^{\mathrm{rank}}$ digits. Its constraints are charged as well as its digits. The constraint PIOP, a sum-check over the committed table, costs about <!--fig:constraint_term_mults-->9.8<!--/fig--> word multiplications per product term of a constraint, the measured rate of the Booleanity sum-check. A nonzero of a linear form costs <!--fig:nonzero_small_mults-->2<!--/fig--> for a small constant or <!--fig:nonzero_field_mults-->4<!--/fig--> for a field constant, the forms being over word entries. This is charged for the application's constraints and the merge's alike, and the PIOP's transcript is absorbed and its verifier run in the merge above.

Its claims live in the 64-bit field itself, Akita's commitment modulus, and the verifier recomposes each Pasta value from 32-bit limbs proved as linear claims in the same sum-check. So Kishu does not use the decoupled commitment, and its soundness rests on Akita's own at the 64-bit profile and on the draft's Booleanity certificate. Its opening proof and decider are priced on the Goldilocks variant's plan and measurement, the same profile, at Kishu's digit count.

## Pasta multiplication by two routes

Akita's claims are in one fixed field, so a Pasta multiplication $a \cdot b = k \cdot p + r$ over committed bits cannot be checked modulo that field alone: the prover could choose $r$. Kishu's rows therefore come in two routes, each a row. By the limb route the relation is checked coefficient by coefficient over 29-bit limbs, which are linear in the bits and cost nothing to commit. That is <!--fig:mult_coefficients-->17<!--/fig--> constraints of up to nine product terms each, bounded below half the modulus so that they hold over the integers, with a committed carry of <!--fig:mult_carry_bits-->34<!--/fig--> bits each. A multiplication is then <!--fig:mult_limbs_digits-->1,088<!--/fig--> digits and <!--fig:mult_limbs_terms-->98<!--/fig--> product terms. By the fingerprint route the relation is one constraint in each of <!--fig:fingerprint_instances-->3<!--/fig--> PIOP instances over random 52-bit primes, as BitZ checks it, at <!--fig:bitz_gate_bits-->510<!--/fig--> digits. The price is per step that has multiplications, so a step of hashes alone pays nothing and the routes tie on the path applications. Each instance's evaluation claim becomes about $\sqrt{2^{\mathrm{rank}}}$ integer column sums by BitZ's tensor split, each exact as one claim in the 64-bit field and committed as digits of <!--fig:column_sum_bits-->61<!--/fig--> bits. That is <!--fig:fingerprint_step_digits_21-->265,000<!--/fig--> digits a step at rank 21, with three more transcripts for the merge above. So the recovery circuit is <!--fig:app_recovery_kishu_limbs-->17 million<!--/fig--> digits by the limb route and <!--fig:app_recovery_kishu_fingerprint-->8.5 million<!--/fig--> by the fingerprint route, with constraint PIOPs of <!--fig:piop_recovery_kishu_limbs_s-->0.0120<!--/fig--> and <!--fig:piop_recovery_kishu_fingerprint_s-->0.00409<!--/fig--> s on one core.

## The merge

Kishu's merge is Akita's, costed in digits. A bit gate is one digit, so a BLAKE3 compression of the transcript is <!--fig:blake3_bits-->15,000<!--/fig--> digits and the images' reductions one per bit. The BLAKE3 accounting is its seven rounds of eight G functions:

- each 32-bit addition is one relation $a + b = s + 2^{32} c$ over word entries, with the 32 sum bits and the carry committed;
- each XOR bit is one constraint $z = x + y - 2xy$ with its bit committed;
- rotations are free, being wiring; and
- the finalisation is 16 words of XORs.

That is <!--fig:blake3_bits-->15,000<!--/fig--> digits, <!--fig:blake3_piop_terms-->7,904<!--/fig--> constraints and <!--fig:blake3_piop_nonzeros-->31,616<!--/fig--> nonzeros a compression. The same accounting at 64 bits, each addition in two halves since a 64-bit sum can wrap modulo $q$, gives BLAKE2b <!--fig:blake2b_bits_kishu-->50,944<!--/fig--> digits and <!--fig:blake2b_piop_terms-->26,368<!--/fig--> constraints. Under BLAKE3 every transcript element the prover supplies is first checked to be below the modulus, since a 64-bit decomposition is canonical only there, about <!--fig:range_check_digits-->63<!--/fig--> digits a word.

The merge's arithmetic is over the 64-bit field and its quadratic extension, which is the PIOP's own field. As in BitZ, the constraint PIOP runs over the ring itself, and only the final linear claim is proved on the bits. So a multiplication there is one constraint, with no bit decomposition and no quotient. But every wire is committed as its bits: <!--fig:q64_digits-->64<!--/fig--> digits for a field element and <!--fig:e_mult_digits-->128<!--/fig--> for a product in the extension. So the sum-check's arithmetic is <!--fig:sumcheck_gates-->400<!--/fig--> such products, each circuit's claim <!--fig:claim_gates-->4<!--/fig--> and the Booleanity sum-check's verifier about <!--fig:bool_sumcheck_merge_mults-->3,000<!--/fig-->; the projection verifier scans <!--fig:bool_projection_scan-->3.1 million<!--/fig--> sampled coefficients, a digit each. The transcript's elements and each circuit's claim are elements of the extension, <!--fig:kishu_claim_bytes-->16<!--/fig--> bytes, and the Booleanity sum-check's own transcript is absorbed with them.

Under the Poseidon option the transcript is hashed by Poseidon over the 64-bit field at width 12 and rate 8 with the $x^3$ S-box: <!--fig:q64_poseidon_mults-->276<!--/fig--> multiplications a permutation at <!--fig:q64_digits-->64<!--/fig--> digits each. That is <!--fig:q64_poseidon_over_blake3_pct-->17<!--/fig-->% more committed digits than BLAKE3 per absorbed byte. A permutation is <!--fig:q64_poseidon_mults-->276<!--/fig--> constraints with <!--fig:poseidon_q64_piop_nonzeros-->13,800<!--/fig--> nonzeros of field constants, where a BLAKE3 compression is <!--fig:blake3_piop_terms-->7,904<!--/fig--> with <!--fig:blake3_piop_nonzeros-->31,616<!--/fig--> of small ones, and these are charged at the rates above. <!--claim:hash_within_quarter:de8249-->Per 64 bytes absorbed, commitments and constraints together, BLAKE3 costs <!--fig:blake3_64b_mults-->470,000<!--/fig--> word multiplications and Poseidon <!--fig:poseidon_64b_mults-->430,000<!--/fig-->, within <!--fig:hash_64b_within_pct-->11<!--/fig-->% of each other, which many modelling choices could sway either way.<!--/claim--> So both hashes are kept as variants of Kishu on the frontier page, each a family of its own in the list and the plot. The BLAKE3 rows appear under either hash option and the Poseidon rows under the arithmetic-hash option, and the frontier decides between them.

## The recovery circuit

The Ironwood recovery circuit itself is hash-heavy: its expensive parts are seven BLAKE2b-512 compressions (the cost section of [\[ZIP-2005\]](#ref-zip2005); beside two Sinsemilla commitments, two full-width Pallas scalar multiplications, a Merkle path, and a BLAKE3 compression in the quantum-key signature). In Kishu a Boolean hash is one bit per wire against many Pasta gates per round in the others, and the integer arithmetic simulates $\mathbb{F}_p$ without a bit decomposition per operation, with range checks free. Costed component by component in this arithmetization, with a hash gate at one digit and a multiplication at <!--fig:mult_limbs_digits-->1,088<!--/fig--> digits by the limb route or <!--fig:bitz_gate_bits-->510<!--/fig--> by the fingerprint route, the recovery circuit is <!--fig:app_recovery_kishu_limbs-->17 million<!--/fig--> or <!--fig:app_recovery_kishu_fingerprint-->8.5 million<!--/fig--> digits. In Pasta arithmetic it is <!--fig:app_recovery_pasta_poseidon-->570,000<!--/fig--> gates. The frontier page's transaction measure uses those counts together with the constraint PIOP's work.

## Differences from Ragu

Ragu's arithmetic circuits are over the Pallas base field. A circuit of $n$ gates has four wires a gate, with each gate enforcing $a_i b_i = c_i$ and $c_i d_i = 0$, and $4n$ linear constraints on all the wires. The trace is one structured polynomial of degree below $4n$, committed by Pedersen. The gates and constraints are checked together by one revdot identity at random points, sound by Schwartz–Zippel in the field ([Ragu's arithmetization chapter](https://tachyon.z.cash/ragu/protocol/local/arithmetization.html)). A step of rank $r$ commits $2^r$ coefficients, so $2^{r-2}$ gates: at rank 13, Ragu's production rank, 2,048 gates, which every one of Tachyon's circuits fits. Kishu differs in these ways:

- **The witness is bits.** Ragu's wire holds a Pallas element; Kishu's digit holds a bit, so a 255-bit value is 255 digits with its constraints, and a Boolean value costs no decomposition. Ragu's gate costs about one Pallas multiplication of Pedersen work per wire; Kishu's digit costs <!--fig:kishu_digit_mults-->11.0<!--/fig--> word multiplications to commit against about <!--fig:pedersen_per_coeff_13-->5,600<!--/fig--> for a Pedersen coefficient at rank 13.
- **Constraints are a sum-check, not a gate identity.** Ragu's constraints are the fixed gate shape and linear forms, checked by one polynomial identity over the field. Kishu's are a sum-check PIOP over the committed table, as in BitZ and Zinc+'s universal constraint systems, with integer relations projected to the 64-bit field or to random primes. A constraint is charged by its product terms and nonzeros rather than by gates.
- **Hashes are cheap and Pasta arithmetic is dear.** In Ragu a Poseidon permutation over Pallas is about 192 gates and a Pasta multiplication one gate, while a bit-oriented hash would cost a gate per bit operation. In Kishu a BLAKE3 compression is <!--fig:blake3_bits-->15,000<!--/fig--> digits, one per bit, and a Pasta multiplication <!--fig:bitz_gate_bits-->510<!--/fig--> or <!--fig:mult_limbs_digits-->1,088<!--/fig--> digits by relation, since the field is not Pasta's. That is why the recovery circuit is <!--fig:app_recovery_kishu_fingerprint-->8.5 million<!--/fig--> digits against <!--fig:app_recovery_pasta_poseidon-->570,000<!--/fig--> Pasta gates, and why Zoodles, with no Pasta arithmetic, suits it.
- **The claim field is small.** Ragu's claims are Pallas elements; Kishu's are elements of the 64-bit field's quadratic extension, <!--fig:kishu_claim_bytes-->16<!--/fig--> bytes, so a Pasta value is proved through limbs or a fingerprint.
- **The step is larger and its size is a knob.** Ragu's step is fixed at $2^{13}$ coefficients; Kishu's is $2^{\mathrm{rank}}$ digits over a range of ranks, and one Kishu step holds several of Tachyon's circuits, as the [Zoodles](zoodles.md) page's packing shows.
- **The merge verifies a lattice fold.** Ragu's merge circuits verify Pedersen-IPA accumulation with Poseidon transcripts over Pallas ([the frontier page's circuit budget](pareto-template.html#circuit-budget)); Kishu's verify Akita's fold, its sum-checks and images, with a BLAKE3 or Poseidon transcript in digits, as above.
- **Zero knowledge by masking.** Ragu rerandomizes its proofs in two steps of their own; Kishu's accumulation is made zero-knowledge by a masking fold with rejection sampling, paid once per transaction, as the [zero-knowledge note](zk-fiat-shamir-with-aborts.md) describes.

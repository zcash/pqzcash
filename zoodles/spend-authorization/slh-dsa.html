Post-Quantum Zcash

SLH-DSA at NIST Level 3

This page is for illustration only. Zoodles does not adopt SLH-DSA, and nothing here proposes it. The page shows what a spend authorization at NIST’s 192-bit level would cost inside a proof, since a signature scheme at that level compounds its cost with the proof system’s. The frontier page has the matching illustration for the proof system itself.

The signature

SLH-DSA [FIPS205] is the standardized form of SPHINCS+, a stateless hash-based signature. It would authorize a spend exactly as leanSPHINCS does: the signature is made outside the proof over the same transaction digest, and is verified inside the spend’s step. The parameter set considered is SLH-DSA-SHAKE-192s, which the standard places in security category 3. Its hash is SHAKE256, so its verifier in the circuit is Keccak permutations.

Why the small-signature parameter set

The standard has two parameter sets at the level, 192s and 192f. The second signs faster, which a proof does not benefit from, and costs its verifier more. A verifier recomputes one WOTS+ public key for each layer of the hypertree: 51 chains, at half of their 15 steps expected, and the key’s compression. SLH-DSA-SHAKE-192f has 22 layers where SLH-DSA-SHAKE-192s has 7. So 192s takes an expected 3,070 Keccak permutations to verify, for a signature of 16,224 bytes. 192f takes 9,006 permutations, 2.9 times as many, for a signature of 35,664 bytes. Either is far more than leanSPHINCS: 192s takes 12 times its permutations. So 192s is the set considered.

The counts follow from the standard’s parameters. Each call of the hash absorbs the public seed, a 32-byte address, and its input, which is one permutation for a chain step or a tree node. The verifier’s count is an expectation, since the chains’ lengths depend on the message digest.

The cost in the circuit

A Zoodles transaction of two spends by SLH-DSA-SHAKE-192s and two outputs is 283 million of Kishu’s digits. At the frontier page’s default floor, with zero knowledge and a BLAKE3 transcript, Kishu proves it in 8.20 s on one core, in 7 steps of a step of 1.25×225 digits. The same transaction with leanSPHINCS over BLAKE3 takes 0.110 s. The frontier page’s transaction list has the SLH-DSA transaction, so that its cost can be seen under any family and floor.

The signature is by far the largest of the three, since the transaction’s digits are almost all the two verifiers’.

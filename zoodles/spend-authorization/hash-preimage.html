Post-Quantum Zcash

Hash preimage

The simplest authorization method is Sprout’s: the note’s payment key is a hash of the authorizing key, and the spender proves knowledge of the preimage. The preimage and its image are a quantum spending key and a quantum key as ZIP 2005 defines them [ZIP-2005], qk=Hqk(qsk) by the ZIP’s BLAKE3 derivation.1 Each protocol has keys of its own; what they share is the derivation, so the same check serves:

  • a native Zoodles spend, where qk is the owner component of the note’s payment key;
  • the recovery of an Ironwood note on the use_qsk=true path, where it is the arm SoKqsk of the Recovery Statement; and
  • the recovery of a Tachyon note, once its proof-authorizing key carries qk.

In Zoodles the spend step opens the payment key to (nk,qk), one BLAKE3 compression. The check, that qk is derived from qsk, is a second, in an authorization node that every spend from that key uses, so it is proved once for each spending key (Spend authorization). On its own the statement is one BLAKE3 compression, 11,344 committed digits in Kishu, which the frontier page prices as the device statement qk, with the linking commitment of ZIP 2005’s hardware-wallet section left out for now. Hardware wallets gives what it costs a wallet to prove by itself.

Inside a transaction’s own proof the node that proves the check is bound to the rest of the transaction by the proof’s graph, directly and through the PCD headers. That binding holds only while the node’s accumulator stays with the prover: one that leaked would authorize any transaction it were built into. So in Zoodles the check is a signature in every case, on the transaction’s digest. It is one already where the proof is made outside the transaction’s own, as a wallet’s leaf handed to the host or as ZIP 2005’s SoKqsk on the transaction’s SigHash. The rest of this page is about what a signature requires.

The signature of knowledge

A proof of knowledge of a preimage of qk is not enough. It would verify under any transaction, so it could be replayed; the statement has to be signed. The reason goes deeper than replay. The reduction that proves SoKqsk unforgeable, as a signature scheme with key qk, has to answer signing queries without qsk. So it simulates them, and it must then extract a preimage from a forger who has seen simulated proofs. That is simulation extractability, which plain knowledge soundness does not give. A signature of knowledge is by definition a simulation-extractable proof with the message as its label [CL06].

The folklore technique, and where it is proved

The technique is to bind the message into the Fiat–Shamir transcript from the start. The instance and the message go into the hash before the first challenge, and the chain of challenges binds everything after. Classically this is proved. For sigma protocols it is [FKMV12]. For multi-round sum-check protocols it is recent. [DG23] shows Spartan and Bulletproofs simulation-extractable with no change. [FFKRZ23] and [KPT23] prove it for Fiat–Shamir of a polynomial IOP with a binding commitment in general, under the conditions one expects: the instance hashed first, and a unique-response or state-restoration property of the protocol. The Frozen Heart vulnerabilities of 2022 [TOB22], forgeries against implementations that left the instance out of the hash, are what omitting it costs. The technique is free.

What is missing is the quantum random-oracle model, which Zoodles and the Recovery Protocol need. Fiat–Shamir of sigma protocols is settled there, by [DFMS19] and [LZ19]. Knowledge soundness of Fiat–Shamir on an IOP is settled with polynomial loss, by [CMS19]. But the multi-round measure-and-reprogram bound [DFM20] loses a factor exponential in the number of rounds, which for thirty sum-check rounds says nothing. And the simulation-extractability proofs above rewind, which the quantum setting does not allow. We know of no simulation-extractability result for a sum-check-based succinct argument in the quantum random-oracle model with usable bounds. In the trust-boundary style of the Ironwood formalization, the signature-of-knowledge property would have to be a named hypothesis.

A route that is available: the non-injectivity of the hash

A classical argument avoids simulation extractability altogether, and it fits this statement unusually well. Hqk maps 256 bits to 256 bits, so a uniformly random qsk has a second preimage with probability about 1−1/e, 63 percent. The reduction to the collision resistance of Hqk picks qsk itself, answers every signing query with the real prover, and extracts a preimage from the forgery. A zero-knowledge proof is witness indistinguishable [FS90]. So when a second preimage exists, the forger’s view is the same whichever preimage the reduction holds, and the extracted one differs from the reduction’s with probability about a half. Then the reduction holds a collision. The loss is a factor of about 3.2. This is the two-witness form that [KW03] used for tight signature reductions, with the hash’s own non-injectivity standing in for the second key, so the circuit does not change. It needs zero knowledge and knowledge soundness with auxiliary input, the honest signatures on other messages being that input. Both are available in the quantum random-oracle model for the proof systems in play, and the message is still bound into the instance before the first challenge. Nothing in the protocol changes; only the proof does. This is the proof to write for the Kishu route. The hash-based signature route does not raise the question. leanSPHINCS is a signature scheme with its own strong unforgeability proof, 127 classical bits in the random-oracle model [Wam26], and the message enters its digest with the randomizer.
